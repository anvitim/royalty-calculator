# Who gets paid when a song is streamed? (UK)

**Who this is for:** a self-released UK artist who writes their own songs, has no record label or publisher, and uses a distributor (a company that gets the music onto Spotify, Apple Music and other services).

**FACT** means the figure is stated in the linked source. **ASSUMPTION** means a typical figure, an estimate, or a source figure applied to a situation it doesn't directly cover; each one says which.

A song earns money in two ways: from **the recording** (the audio file, money to the artist) and from **the songwriting** (the words and melody, money to the songwriter). Here, the artist and songwriter are the same person.

## 1. What one UK stream is worth before any split

The service pools its income and divides it by each track's share of all streams on that service in that country. **FACT:** [CMA report, footnote 89](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)

There are two ways to estimate the value of one stream, both using 2024 UK figures. Both divide by 199.6 billion UK audio streams. **FACT:** [BPI](https://www.bpi.co.uk/news/12870360)

| Working | Calculation | Per stream |
|---|---|---|
| A. What listeners spent: £2.02bn on music streaming services. **FACT:** [ERA via Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/era-uk-music-consumption-hits-new-high-with-record-breaking-results-for-volume-and-revenue-in-2024/091158) | £2.02bn ÷ 199.6bn | **1.0p** |
| A without VAT, if the £2.02bn includes 20% VAT. **ASSUMPTION:** the source doesn't say | 1.0p ÷ 1.2 | **0.84p** |
| B. What record labels received: £1.02bn from streaming. **FACT:** [BPI via Record of the Day](https://recordoftheday.com/on-the-move/news-press/uk-recorded-music-market-reached-a-decade-of-continuous-growth-in-2024-as-annual-streaming-revenue-surpassed-1-billion-for-the-first-time) Then scaled up, assuming the recording is 53% of the total (see section 2). | £1.02bn ÷ 199.6bn ÷ 0.53 | **0.96p** |

**Result: about 0.8p–1.0p per stream on average. ASSUMPTION**, because:
- the sources count slightly different things (B's revenue includes video streaming, but the stream count is audio only);
- the 53% figure comes from 2021, not 2024.

**Why individual streams vary:**
- Paid tiers earn "considerably more revenue" than free, ad-funded tiers. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.93](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)
- Because each service splits its own pool, a stream is worth more on a service whose listeners pay more or play fewer songs. **ASSUMPTION:** this follows from the pooling rule above.

**Check against the CMA's £12,000 for 12 million streams (0.1p a stream).** Using data from the major labels, the CMA estimated what 12 million UK streams a year would earn a recording artist. Publishing and overseas income are excluded. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.66](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)

That's about a fifth of the recording share worked out above, which is 53% of 0.8p–1.0p, or 0.42p–0.53p.

The CMA also found that royalty rates in major-label deals averaged 23.3% in 2021. **FACT:** [CMA summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-and-streaming-market-study-final-report/executive-summary)

**ASSUMPTION:** the £12,000 is the artist's slice after the label keeps most of the recording money (about 23% of 0.42p–0.53p ≈ 0.1p). The CMA doesn't spell out the deal behind it. A self-released artist keeps the whole recording share.

## 2. Percentage cuts: follow £1

| Step | Who | Cut | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Streaming service keeps | e.g. Spotify | 32p | **FACT:** UK, 2021 ([CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-and-streaming-market-study-final-report/executive-summary)) |
| Paid for the recording | distributor → artist | 53p | **FACT:** as above |
| Paid for the songwriting | PRS and MCPS → writer | 15p | **FACT:** as above |
| Distributor's cut | DistroKid / CD Baby | 0% / 9% | **FACT:** [DistroKid](https://distrokid.com/pricing/), [CD Baby](https://cdbaby.com/cd-baby-cost/) |
| Songwriting money split | PRS (performance) / MCPS (copying) | about half each | **ASSUMPTION:** standard for streaming, not confirmed from a source |
| PRS running costs | PRS | 9.3% | **ASSUMPTION:** this was PRS's cost across *all* its income in 2022 ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/Newsroom/society-news/prs-music-reports-record-breaking-revenues-and-distributions-collecting-ps964)), not a streaming-specific rate |
| MCPS commission | MCPS | 7.4% | **ASSUMPTION:** this was MCPS's average across *all* its income in 2021 ([PRS for Music](https://www.prsformusic.com/press/2022/mcps-announces-2021-distributions-of-181-million-to-its-members)), not a streaming-specific rate |

**Other rules:**
- On Spotify, a track earns recording money only after 1,000 streams in 12 months. **FACT:** [Spotify](https://artists.spotify.com/royalties-guide)
- With no publisher, PRS pays the writer the publisher's share too. **ASSUMPTION:** based on PRS guidance; confirm with PRS.

**Result:** using DistroKid, an artist-writer keeps **about 67p of every £1**:
- 53p from the recording;
- about 14p from the songwriting, after PRS and MCPS costs.

## 4. Worked examples

These use DistroKid and membership of both PRS and MCPS. **ASSUMPTION:** 0.9p per stream, the middle of section 1's range, so the artist-writer keeps about **0.6p a stream**.

| Streams a year | Earned | Year 1 after fixed costs (£219) | Later years (£19) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | about £60 | **about −£159** (a loss) | about +£41 |
| 1,000,000 | about £6,000 | about +£5,790 | about +£5,990 |

**Not covered here:** PPL, which pays for radio and public play but not on-demand streams ([PPL](https://www.ppluk.com/royalties/the-rights-ppl-can-collect-for/)); YouTube; and streams outside the UK.
