# Who gets paid when a song is streamed? (UK)

**Who this is for:** a UK artist who writes their own songs and has no publisher.
- **Sections 1–4:** the artist is self-released, with no record label, and uses a distributor (a company that gets the music onto Spotify, Apple Music and other services).
- **Section 5:** the same artist signed to an independent label.

**FACT** means the figure is stated in the linked source. **ASSUMPTION** means a typical figure, an estimate, or a source figure applied to a situation it doesn't directly cover; each one says which.

A song earns money in two ways: from **the recording** (the audio file, money to the artist) and from **the songwriting** (the words and melody, money to the songwriter). Here, the artist and songwriter are the same person.

## 1. What one UK stream is worth before any split

The service pools its income and divides it by each track's share of all streams on that service in that country. **FACT:** [CMA report, footnote 89](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)

There are two ways to estimate the value of one stream, both using 2024 UK figures. Both divide by 199.6 billion UK audio streams. **FACT:** [BPI](https://www.bpi.co.uk/news/12870360)

| Working | Calculation | Per stream |
|---|---|---|
| A. What listeners spent: £2.02bn on music streaming services. **FACT:** [ERA via Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/era-uk-music-consumption-hits-new-high-with-record-breaking-results-for-volume-and-revenue-in-2024/091158) | £2.02bn ÷ 199.6bn | **1.0p** |
| A without VAT, if the £2.02bn includes 20% VAT. **ASSUMPTION:** the source doesn't say | 1.0p ÷ 1.2 | **0.84p** |
| B. What record labels received: £1.02bn from streaming. **FACT:** [BPI via Record of the Day](https://recordoftheday.com/on-the-move/news-press/uk-recorded-music-market-reached-a-decade-of-continuous-growth-in-2024-as-annual-streaming-revenue-surpassed-1-billion-for-the-first-time) Then scaled up, assuming the recording is 53% of the total (see section 2). | £1.02bn ÷ 199.6bn ÷ 0.53 | **0.96p** |

**Result: about 0.8p–1.0p per stream on average. ASSUMPTION**, because:
- the sources count slightly different things (B's revenue includes video streaming, but the stream count is audio only);
- the 53% figure comes from 2021, not 2024.

**Why individual streams vary:**
- Paid tiers earn "considerably more revenue" than free, ad-funded tiers. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.93](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)
- Because each service splits its own pool, a stream is worth more on a service whose listeners pay more or play fewer songs. **ASSUMPTION:** this follows from the pooling rule above.

**Check against the CMA's £12,000 for 12 million streams (0.1p a stream).** Using data from the major labels, the CMA estimated what 12 million UK streams a year would earn a recording artist. Publishing and overseas income are excluded. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.66](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)

That's about a fifth of the recording share worked out above, which is 53% of 0.8p–1.0p, or 0.42p–0.53p.

The CMA also found that royalty rates in major-label deals averaged 23.3% in 2021. **FACT:** [CMA summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-and-streaming-market-study-final-report/executive-summary)

**ASSUMPTION:** the £12,000 is the artist's slice after the label keeps most of the recording money (about 23% of 0.42p–0.53p ≈ 0.1p). The CMA doesn't spell out the deal behind it. A self-released artist keeps the whole recording share.

| MCPS commission | MCPS | 7.4% | **ASSUMPTION:** this was MCPS's average across *all* its income in 2021 ([PRS for Music](https://www.prsformusic.com/press/2022/mcps-announces-2021-distributions-of-181-million-to-its-members)), not a streaming-specific rate |

**Other rules:**
- On Spotify, a track earns recording money only after 1,000 streams in 12 months. **FACT:** [Spotify](https://artists.spotify.com/royalties-guide)
- With no publisher, PRS pays the writer the publisher's share too. **ASSUMPTION:** based on PRS guidance; confirm with PRS.

**Result:** using DistroKid, an artist-writer keeps **about 67p of every £1**:
- 53p from the recording;
- about 14p from the songwriting, after PRS and MCPS costs.

## 5. Signed to an independent label

An independent ("indie") label is a record company that isn't one of the three majors (Universal, Sony and Warner). UK examples include Beggars Group, BMG and Domino. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.23](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf)

**Sources used:**
- **IPO:** [*Music creators' earnings in the digital era*](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/614c760fd3bf7f719095b5ad/music-creators-earnings-report.pdf), Intellectual Property Office, 2021.
- **DCMS:** [*Economics of music streaming*](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm), House of Commons DCMS Committee, 2021.

### What changes from self-released

- **The label, not the artist, collects the recording money.** The label pays the artist a share of it.
- **The songwriting side doesn't change.** The 15p in every £1 still goes through PRS and MCPS to the artist, as in section 2. **ASSUMPTION:** this assumes the artist hasn't also signed a publishing deal.
- **The artist has no distributor fee to pay.** The label pays for distribution.

### Two deal types

- **Royalty deal:** the artist gets a percentage of the label's income from their music. The label's costs are paid back from **the artist's percentage only**. **FACT:** [DCMS, para 45](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm)
- **Profit-share deal:** the label's costs are paid back from **all** the income first, and the profit is then shared. Indie profit shares are "usually 50:50". **FACT:** [DCMS, paras 44–45](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm)
- **Both are common.** Of 83 recording contracts the IPO examined, 41 were "some variation on this 50-50 deal". Most of the 83 came from smaller independent companies. **FACT:** [IPO, pp. 144 and 147](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/614c760fd3bf7f719095b5ad/music-creators-earnings-report.pdf)

### The cost of making and promoting the release

**£5,000** covers recording, artwork, a video and marketing, with no cash advance (money paid to the artist before release). **ASSUMPTION:** a small release. Real costs vary widely.

The comparison uses the same £5,000 in every deal. What changes is who pays it, and who carries the loss if the music doesn't earn it back.

| Deal | Who pays the £5,000 | How it's paid back | If the music never earns it back |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-released | the artist, upfront, from their own money | from all the artist's earnings | **the artist** loses the money |
| Royalty deal | the label | only from the artist's royalty | **the label** has lost the money. The shortfall stays on the artist's account and is taken from any future royalties |
| Profit-share deal | the label | from all the label's income, before any profit is shared | as for the royalty deal |

- **Taking costs back from earnings is called "recoupment".** The label recovers costs from the artist's royalties, not from the artist's pocket. **FACT:** [CMA report, para 2.26](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6384f43ee90e077898ccb48e/Music_and_streaming_final_report.pdf) (costs are "refunded from those royalties")
- **The artist never has to repay out of pocket. ASSUMPTION:** this is the usual position, but check the individual contract.
- **Some labels cancel old shortfalls.** 19% of independent labels in the trade body AIM write off an unpaid balance, after ten years on average; Beggars does so after 15 years. Many labels don't. **FACT:** [DCMS, para 46](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm)
- **In real deals, a profit share often counts more costs than a royalty deal**, so it can take longer to pay back. **FACT:** [IPO, p. 147](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/614c760fd3bf7f719095b5ad/music-creators-earnings-report.pdf)
- **This comparison uses the same £5,000 for both deal types. ASSUMPTION:** it keeps the comparison simple.

### Cash advance (optional)

A cash advance is money the label pays the artist upfront, before the release has earned anything. It isn't a gift. It's added to the release costs, and the total is paid back from the artist's earnings in the same way.

- **Royalty deal:** the advance is paid back from the artist's royalty only. **FACT:** "the performer's advance and any negotiated recoupable costs are earned out from the performer's royalties only" ([DCMS, para 45](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm))
- **Profit-share deal:** the advance counts as one of the costs, paid back from all income before the profit is shared. **ASSUMPTION:** the sources describe how costs are paid back in a profit share but don't single out the advance.
- **The artist keeps the advance** even if the music never earns it back. **ASSUMPTION:** as above, the usual position, but check the individual contract.
- **Default: £0. ASSUMPTION.** The worked examples in this section don't include one.

**Example:** a £2,000 advance with 1 million streams.
- Year 1 becomes about **+£3,037** in either label deal: the £1,037 from the table below, plus the £2,000 advance.
- More is still owed to the label: **£6,046** in the royalty deal and **£3,184** in the profit share.
- The artist waits longer to be paid for the recording: about **7.3 million streams** in the royalty deal (£7,000 to pay back at 25p in the pound) and about **1.8 million** in the profit share.

| Songwriting | PRS and MCPS → writer | 15p, minus costs | as section 2 |

**Per stream at 0.9p:**
- The label receives about **0.38p** (53% of 0.9p, minus the distributor's 20%).
- **Royalty deal:** the artist's share is about **0.095p** (25% of 0.38p).
- **Profit-share deal:** once costs are paid back, the artist gets about **0.19p** (50% of 0.38p).
- **Self-released:** the artist keeps the whole **0.48p** recording share.
- **Every deal:** songwriting adds about **0.12p**.

### Why the royalty deal takes four times as long

- **Royalty deal:** the £5,000 is paid back only from the artist's 25% slice. Of every £1 the label receives, only 25p goes towards the costs; the label keeps the other 75p. To pay back £5,000 at 25p in the pound, the label must receive **£20,000**, four times the costs. By then it has kept £15,000 for itself on top of getting its £5,000 back. That takes about **5.2 million streams**.
- **Profit-share deal:** the costs come out of every £1 the label receives. Once the label has received **£5,000**, the costs are covered and the 50/50 split begins. That takes about **1.3 million streams**.
- **Why exactly four times:** 25% is a quarter. A royalty rate of 20% would mean five times as long.

The DCMS Committee gave the same example: with £10,000 of costs and a 25% royalty, income must pass £40,000 (£30,000 of it to the label) before the artist is paid. **FACT:** [DCMS, para 45](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5802/cmselect/cmcumeds/50/5006.htm)

**When a self-released artist gets back what they spent.** A self-released artist keeps their songwriting money too, so everything they keep counts towards what they spent. That's the £5,000 release, plus £219 of year-1 fixed costs (DistroKid about £19, PRS and MCPS £200).

- They keep about **0.60p a stream**: 0.477p recording (53% of 0.9p) plus about 0.124p songwriting (15% of 0.9p, after PRS and MCPS costs).
- £5,219 ÷ 0.60p ≈ **869,000 streams** (868,779, rounding up to a whole stream) before year 1 stops being a loss.
- This matches the table below: at 1 million streams they're about £788 ahead.
- **ASSUMPTION:** this uses the 0.9p from section 4.

**When the artist is no longer out of pocket in a label deal.** Until the label's costs are paid back, a label-deal artist earns only the songwriting money, about 0.124p a stream. That has to cover the £200 joining fees: £200 ÷ 0.124p ≈ **162,000 streams**. A cash advance of £200 or more covers the fees from the start.

### Fixed costs

- **Every deal:** the PRS and MCPS joining fees, £200 once, as in section 3.
- **Self-released only:** DistroKid's yearly fee as well (about £19).

### Worked examples: like-for-like (year 1, 0.9p per stream, £5,000 release costs)

| Streams | Self-released (artist pays £5,000) | Royalty deal (label pays £5,000) | Profit-share deal (label pays £5,000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | £60 earned − £219 fees − £5,000 = **about −£5,159** | £0 recording + £12 songwriting − £200 = **about −£188**. Still owed to the label: £4,990 | £0 + £12 − £200 = **about −£188**. Still owed: £4,962 |
| 1,000,000 | £6,007 − £219 − £5,000 = **about +£788** | £0 + £1,237 − £200 = **about +£1,037**. Still owed: £4,046 | £0 + £1,237 − £200 = **about +£1,037**. Still owed: £1,184 |
| 10,000,000 | £60,073 − £219 − £5,000 = **about +£54,854** | £4,540 + £12,373 − £200 = **about +£16,713** | £16,580 + £12,373 − £200 = **about +£28,753** |

**What the table shows:**
- **At low streams,** a label deal protects the artist. The label, not the artist, carries the £5,000 loss.
- **At high streams,** self-released pays far more, because the artist keeps the whole recording share.
- **Not measured:** what a label adds in return, such as marketing and contacts. That could mean more streams in the first place.
